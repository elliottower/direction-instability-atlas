Direction instability (DI) measures whether a perturbation's effects point the same way across contexts, as the mean pairwise cosine dissimilarity among its signatures. DI is the complement of pairwise phase consistency (Vinck et al. 2010, NeuroImage); this deposit applies it at atlas scale. It provides magnitude-corrected DI with bootstrap confidence intervals for 8,949 LINCS L1000 drugs, 379 Tahoe-100M drugs, 7,946 JUMP-CP CRISPR knockouts, and 12,590 JUMP-CP ORF overexpressions, alongside 25,254 JUMP-CP compounds from the companion deposit.

Eleven hypotheses were pre-registered across three frozen analysis plans (commit SHAs 0d07a01, a17c125, 4c34699) before computation. Three pass and eight fail. The principal positive result is that drug mechanism-of-action class explains 20% of residualized DI variance (eta-squared = 0.201, permutation p < 0.001), separating core-machinery inhibitors (mTOR, HDAC; low DI) from receptor-signaling drugs (serotonin and histamine agonists; high DI). DI is concordant across measurement modalities (partial rho = 0.19, n = 378). Knockout and overexpression DI for the same gene are unrelated (rho = 0.011, n = 5,220), and expression variance, paralog count and paralog expression breadth each explain under 0.4% of DI variance after confound control.

DI computed over JUMP-CP plate replicates indexes technical reproducibility within a single cell line rather than context-dependence across cell types. On those values essentiality breadth predicts lower DI, the opposite of the registered prediction, at partial rho = -0.332, attenuating to rho = -0.189 once signal strength is also controlled; the direction replicates on a two-cell-line CRISPR pilot (rho = -0.264, n = 128).

The deposit contains the atlas data files (CSV), all experiment result files (JSON), the three frozen pre-registration documents, the full analysis codebase (Python), and the manuscript.
